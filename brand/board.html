Ghosh Designs Identity concepts Proof sheet 01

Three ways to draw a G.

Every route below starts from the same letter and the same locked name. What changes is the argument each one makes about how the studio works. Marks are true vector geometry — no raster, no AI image, single-ink capable, legible down to 16 px.

A

The Seal

Editorial luxury

A high-contrast didone G struck inside a double-hairline ring. A seal is what a studio puts on work once it is finished — it reads as authorship and approval, not decoration. The thick–thin modulation comes from a narrowed inner counter, the same way a real Didone gets its contrast.

B

The Aperture

Dark precision

A perfect monoline circle stays a circle until one deliberate cut is made into it. That cut is the crossbar, and it is the only element carrying colour. The argument: raw material is neutral, and the decision is the design. It is also the cheapest mark here to animate — the bar can draw itself in on load.

C

The Counter

Bold grotesk

A heavy circular G whose counter — the empty space inside the letter — has been squared off. Design as the act of putting structure inside something organic, with the idea living in the part you didn't draw. The one mark here that survives being stamped, engraved or embroidered in a single ink with nothing lost.

Reading the sheet

If you want my call

B, The Aperture. A design studio's mark has to sit next to client work all day without competing with it, and B is the only one of the three that stays quiet while still carrying an idea you can say out loud in one sentence. It is also the most flexible: it animates, it works as a favicon, and dropping the accent gives you a mono version for free.

  • Take A if most of what people touch is physical — cards, folders, printed proposals — and you want the brand to feel established rather than current.
  • Take C if you want to be recognised from across a room and are willing to be the loudest thing on the page to get it.
