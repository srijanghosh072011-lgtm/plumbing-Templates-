10 Quiet Signs You Have a Plumbing Leak Behind a Wall

Most hidden leaks announce themselves months before the drywall stains. Here is what to watch and the one meter test that settles it.

June 18, 2026 6 min read Reviewed by a licensed journeyperson

The short answer

The most reliable way to confirm a hidden leak is the water meter test: shut off every fixture in the house, note the meter reading, wait one hour without using water, then read it again. Any movement means water is escaping somewhere in the system. Common early warning signs before that point include an unexplained bill increase, a musty smell with no visible water, warm spots on the floor, and a water heater that cycles when nothing is running.

Why hidden leaks matter more than obvious ones

A dripping tap is annoying but harmless — you can see it, so you fix it. A leak inside a wall cavity or under a slab is the expensive one precisely because nothing tells you it is there. By the time a stain appears on drywall, water has usually been escaping for weeks or months, and the damage is no longer just the pipe.

That delay is also what causes insurance friction. Most Canadian policies cover sudden water damage but exclude damage from gradual seepage that went unaddressed. The longer a leak runs undetected, the more likely an adjuster classifies it as gradual.

The ten signs, in the order they usually appear

These roughly follow the sequence we see on service calls, from earliest to most advanced:

  • Water bill climbs with no change in household habits — often the very first signal.
  • The water meter creeps when every fixture is off.
  • A musty or earthy smell in one room that does not clear with ventilation.
  • Reduced pressure at one fixture while the rest of the house is normal.
  • A warm patch on a floor, which usually means a hot-water slab leak.
  • The water heater or recirculation pump cycling while nobody is using water.
  • Faint running-water sound in a wall when the house is quiet.
  • Paint or wallpaper bubbling, or baseboards cupping away from the wall.
  • Visible mould in a corner with no obvious moisture source.
  • Foundation cracking or a persistently soggy patch of lawn over the service line.

How to run the meter test properly

Turn off every tap, appliance and ice maker, and tell everyone in the house not to use water for the duration. Find your meter and note the exact reading, including the small sweep hand or leak indicator triangle if it has one. Wait a full hour, then read it again.

Any change at all means water moved. If the leak indicator spins even slowly with everything closed, you have a live leak. This test costs nothing and settles the question definitively, which is why it is worth doing before calling anyone.

What happens when you call us

We locate leaks acoustically and thermally rather than by opening walls speculatively. Acoustic equipment picks up the specific frequency pressurised water makes escaping a pipe, and thermal imaging shows temperature differentials through drywall. Between the two we can usually pin a leak to within a few centimetres.

That matters financially: exploratory demolition to find a leak often costs more than the repair itself. Locating first means one small access point instead of a wall.

Related questions

Can a hidden leak stop on its own?

No. Pressurised supply leaks only get worse as the opening erodes. What can change is whether you notice it — a leak draining into a floor cavity or out to soil may stop showing symptoms while continuing to run, which is the most expensive scenario of all.

How much water does a small hidden leak waste?

A pinhole leak in a pressurised line can waste 900 to 1,800 litres a day. That is enough to show clearly on a bill, which is why an unexplained increase is such a reliable early signal.
